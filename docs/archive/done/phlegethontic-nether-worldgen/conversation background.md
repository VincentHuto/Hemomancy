The strongest answer is to make Phlegethon a place first, then allow the player to bottle, study, and ultimately reproduce its principle. That gives the name proper weight. If “Phlegethon” is merely attached to a fire spell or potion, Dante’s river is reduced to label-work.
A small correction: the usual spelling is Phlegethon, from Greek phlegethōn, “the blazing one.”
Recommended structure: place → draught → manipulation
1. The Phlegethontic Vein
A rare subterranean river or arterial network beneath the Nether—preferably cutting through basalt deltas and beneath fortresses.
It should not simply be red lava. Make it visibly blood subjected to infernal circulation:
- Dark arterial red rather than luminous orange.
- Thick, rolling currents with fatty yellow foam and black coagulated islands.
- Periodic “heartbeats” that accelerate the current and raise the fluid level.
- Bones, armor, and half-preserved shapes moving beneath its surface.
- Vein-like tributaries penetrating netherrack walls.
- The river scalds, inflicts Blood Loss, and partially ignores ordinary Fire Resistance because the injury is partly circulatory rather than thermal.
Lore-wise, this need not be Dante’s literal afterlife. Hemomancers named it Phlegethon after recognizing the same principle: blood made into both medium and punishment. Perhaps the Nether contains several fragments of this greater current rather than one globally continuous river.
This fits your progression matrix very neatly: first Nether establishes blood refinement, while Nether-fortress progression permits genuinely offensive and costly manipulation.
2. Phlegethontic Ichor
The river yields a reagent, but collecting it should be troublesome.
Ordinary buckets fail—the blood clots into useless Cindered Gore when removed. Proper extraction requires:
- A hematic or vitreous vessel.
- Blood Projection into the vessel to establish sympathetic circulation.
- Possibly blaze-derived heat regulation.
- Collection during the river’s pulse rather than between beats.
This gives you a properly physical, early clinical procedure: cannulating Hell itself.
Phlegethontic Ichor could serve as:
- A Flammeus manipulation catalyst.
- A heat-bearing infusion medium.
- A reagent for advanced Hematic Iron or Bloodlust upgrades.
- Fuel for a brazier that accepts neither ordinary blood nor ordinary flame.
- A means of producing “boiled” blood products without merely using a furnace.
3. Draught of the Burning Course
Rather than simple Fire Resistance, this draught makes the drinker’s circulation imitate the river.
For perhaps 60–90 seconds:
- Sprinting leaves short-lived bloody flames.
- Melee attackers are scorched by expelled blood.
- The player becomes highly resistant to fire.
- Blood-based actions deal additional Flammeus damage.
- The player continuously accumulates Phlegethontic Fever.
The important part is the price: remaining still lets the blood boil internally. The drinker must keep moving, attacking, or spending blood to vent pressure. If the fever reaches its limit, they suffer damage and violently eject a blood-fire burst.
Thus the draught is not “drink for fire buff”; it is voluntary circulatory crisis. That is far more Hemomancy.
The manipulation: Course of Phlegethon
This is an excellent candidate for the currently empty Magister/CHARGED runway.
The player holds the cast and projects a crawling river of boiling blood along the ground. It follows terrain, spills down steps, curls around obstacles, and persists briefly as a battlefield hazard.
While channelled:
- Blood drains continuously.
- The current extends farther from the caster.
- Enemies caught within are slowed, scalded, and dragged slightly with its flow.
- Bleeding enemies strengthen or lengthen the current.
- Targets below a health threshold become momentarily “submerged,” taking greater damage.
- Releasing the cast causes the river to coagulate into a low, brittle barrier or a series of black-red clots.
That distinguishes it from vitric_combustion, which is already the Flammeus targeted explosion. Course of Phlegethon would instead be sustained terrain control—the Flammeus school’s grand battlefield manipulation.
The caster should not be wholly safe. Channeling progressively builds Phlegethontic Fever, forcing a choice between extending the river and preserving one’s own veins.
Supporting encounter: The River Wardens
Dante places centaurs along Phlegethon, shooting those who rise too far from the boiling blood. I would echo that without importing the centaurs literally.
Use Sagittarian Drudges or Cautery Wardens stationed on the banks:
- Long, surgically elongated lower bodies suited to traversing the riverbanks.
- Living bows or lance-like arterial projectors.
- They attack anything that climbs out of the current.
- Their shots knock victims back toward the river.
- Some bear brands corresponding to differing depths of punishment.
This becomes a memorable environmental encounter: crossing the river is not merely fire-resistance preparation; the player must contend with custodians deliberately returning them to it.
Secondary uses worth retaining
A few smaller references could reinforce the concept without diluting it:
- Phlegethontic Cautery: a consumable surgical tool that immediately stops Blood Loss but converts part of it into delayed burn damage.
- Phlegethon Glass: translucent black-red glass made by cooling the river, with trapped currents moving inside it.
- River-Smut: coagulated residue used in Flammeus catalysts and Victorian medicinal preparations.
- Phlegethontic Infusion mode: an infusion-chair procedure that heats donated blood before returning it, temporarily empowering fire manipulations while imposing fever and cardiac strain.
- Achievement: Not One Drop of Water for crossing a Phlegethontic Vein without Fire Resistance.
- Mnemonic inquiry: the player learns that the river does not burn because it is hot; it is hot because it remembers every violent heartbeat poured into it.
My recommendation, sir: implement the Phlegethontic Vein, its collectible Ichor, and the charged Course of Phlegethon as one connected content chain. The draught can follow. Then Phlegethon becomes a discovered natural horror, a harvested medical substance, and finally a learned act of will—the world, the laboratory, and the body repeating the same dreadful principle.

Very good, sir. The Nuckelavee is a far stronger anatomical foundation than an ordinary centaur. Dante supplies the office—armed wardens policing the depth of Phlegethon—while Orcadian folklore supplies the appalling flesh.
I would call them the Excoriated Sagittaries.
They should resemble neither a man riding a horse nor a cleanly fused centaur. The “rider” is an excessive human torso erupting from the equine spine, as though horse and executioner were grown from one condemned circulatory system:
- No skin anywhere; black-red muscle glistens beneath translucent fascia.
- Veins stand above the musculature like cords, glowing brighter as they draw heat from the river.
- The human torso has disproportionately long arms, permitting it to draw a great bow across the whole width of the horse-body.
- Its legs are absent—not hidden, but anatomically never formed.
- The equine head hangs low and blind while the human head watches the banks.
- Both heads breathe through the same exposed, bellows-like lungs along the ribs.
- Phlegethontic blood continually pours through fissures in the body, only to be replenished through the hooves.
The bow itself should be biological: two recurved ribs joined by a braided arterial string. Its arrows are calcified thrombi—long clots hardened into barbed shafts.
Their proper behavior
The Sagittaries do not simply attack trespassers. They enforce the river.
- They patrol through boiling blood with only their upper bodies visible.
- When a victim attempts to leave, they fire Recall Barbs that tether and drag it back.
- Targets standing too high above the current receive ordinary piercing volleys.
- Targets submerged too deeply are largely ignored, reflecting the graded punishments of Dante’s Phlegethon.
- If approached in melee, the creature rears and expels a cloud of scalding, septic breath—the Nuckelavee influence coming properly to the fore.
- During the river’s heartbeat, the Sagittary’s exposed vessels inflate and its firing speed increases.
- It weakens rapidly away from Phlegethontic fluid: its muscles blacken, its joints stiffen, and its body begins clotting alive.
That last limitation is important. It makes them feel like organs belonging to the river, not generic Nether monsters that happen to spawn nearby.
A particularly gruesome attack: Arterial Sounding
The Sagittary fires a tether directly into the target. Instead of immediately pulling them, it uses the tether to “measure” their blood:
- High blood volume causes the target to be dragged deeper into the river.
- Low blood volume causes the creature to reel them toward itself for execution.
- Active bleeding lets the Sagittary track the victim through walls or smoke.
- Severing the tether costs the victim a small amount of blood, as the barb tears free.
This gives the guardian a distinctly Hemomancy-specific intelligence: it does not judge distance alone, but circulation.
Their origin
I would avoid making them a natural species. They should be constructed custodians.
An old Hemomantic account might claim that each Sagittary began as two beings: a violent rider and the beast exhausted beneath him. Phlegethon dissolved the distinction between master and mount, then returned them as one skinless instrument of punishment. Whether that tale is literally true can remain uncertain.
A Mnemonist might offer the colder interpretation:
“It is not a man joined to a horse. It is the memory of pursuit, given enough blood to stand.”

This also prevents too much direct dependence on either Dante or Orcadian folklore. Hemomancy is not importing the Nuckelavee wholesale; it is arriving at a similar horror through its own doctrine of embodied memory.
My preferred final identity would be:
Excoriated Sagittary
The skinless custodian of the Phlegethontic Veins, born wherever the river remembers pursuit.
That is a creature worthy of guarding the Blazing River.
