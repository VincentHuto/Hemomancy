# The Brined Court — Working House Design

Planning record · October 19, 2026 · decisions added October 20, 2026

This document consolidates the direction developed in this conversation. It records a working faction design, not implemented mechanics or a replacement for established Hemomancy canon. Names and concepts remain open to refinement. Concrete ability values, progression gates, and Morphling layer mappings have not been specified.

## Place within Hemomancy

The Brined Court is an offshoot practice in the same sense as the Circus. It is associated with the wider Harbinger tradition but does not move in step with it.

The Court follows a niche form of hemomancy: a biological feeding methodology in which Morphlings are inherently symbiotic. Blood sustains a living partner, and that partner reshapes and sustains the host in return. This is the bodily counterpart to the Circus, whose puppeteering and projection work through the metaphysical and mental aspects of the art.

## What makes them a Court

The eight houses share a history of Morphling utility and cultivation, water-based life, and adaptation to different aquatic habitats. They form the Brined Court because they need ways to communicate between those habitats while maintaining boundaries and civility.

The Court accommodates substantial disagreement. Membership does not require devotion to Marimo or Hydra. Its practical purpose is to keep communities in contact and allow them to coexist across the ocean's layers and niches.

The houses are communities with distinct livelihoods, beliefs, and cultivated bodies. Their tendency alignments help shape those identities; they are not merely eight elemental variants of the same culture.

### Houses as strain keepers

Each house keeps its own Morphling strain. The strain is passed down through generations and cultivated to fit the house's ecological and ideological niche, so a house's body, habitat, and beliefs all grow from its strain.

**Working direction: partner organisms.** Each strain is the fungus living in partnership with a second organism suited to its depth, as lichen pairs a fungus with an alga. Sunburst at the top has a sunlight-feeding algal partner, tying it to Marimo's blooms; the deep strains take vent-chemistry partners, as real vent tubeworms and bone-eating worms rely on symbiotic bacteria. The inner strains shift from sun partners to dark partners with depth.

Powers and stage visuals for all eight are in "Strain powers and appearance." Sunburst and Kinbud carry the most authored identity; the inner six are spectrum strains and lean more heavily on carried-forward mechanics.

### Membership

As a rule of thumb, most members are born into their house. On reaching maturity, a person may willingly migrate to another house that better fits their own personality. They may do so **once**. The move is a significant decision because it cannot be repeated.

## Marimo and Hydra

### Marimo: the power of many

Marimo offers love, energy, power, food, and other support to those who give a little back to him. His strength comes from many lives contributing to a collective that sustains them in return.

His domain extends across the sunlit surface through floating algae and phytoplankton. These are small, communal, photosynthetic organisms that thrive by their numbers rather than individually, the natural expression of what Marimo is. Wherever sunlight reaches the water and these drifting multitudes gather, Marimo's influence is present. This is also why his reach fades as sunlight does.

Blooms serve as a visible marker of Marimo's influence in the world. A spreading bloom shows where he is present and growing.

Association with Marimo weakens with depth. The deeper houses have less contact with him, less commonality with his beliefs, and less direct experience of the support he offers. Their distance and rebellion grow out of that estrangement.

### Hydra: the cultivation of one

Hydra's followers cultivate one supreme organism, aspiring at the end of their lives to become part of it. They willingly surrender their individual identity because they believe strength and power matter above all else, and individuality ultimately gets in the way.

They yearn to contribute only their best and greatest selves. Surrender to Hydra is an end-of-life act. It follows a long life of preparation, when they finally consider themselves worthy of giving themselves to Hydra, and is the intentional culmination of cultivation.

Only the most worthy keep a head. Everyone else who surrenders becomes part of Hydra's body, adding mass and strength without a head of their own.

### Anchoring and true petrification

The Court distinguishes two ways a mature body can become still:

- **Anchoring** is a conscious, reversible decision. A host settles into stone, bone, or a living structure in order to hold, shelter, feed, or rest, and remains able to release that hold.
- **True petrification** is not a choice the host can take back. It ends independent embodiment. Marimo's followers who petrify into his moss are absorbed into his network.

Several houses' mature forms are anchored rather than petrified. Mistaking one for the other is a recurring source of misunderstanding about the Court.

### Lore depth

Only the two outer houses need expansive lore: the Giving Light at the top and the Kindling at the bottom. The six inner houses form a spectrum between those poles. Each needs enough identity to be distinguishable (habitat, belief, Morphling direction, and its lean) but does not need lore at the same depth.

## The houses

### Lux — The Giving Light

**Habitat and settlement:** Shoreline light, photosynthetic shallows, surface algae and phytoplankton blooms, communal gardens, and a lighthouse settlement. This is the Court's welcoming face and the house closest to Marimo.

**Belief:** Everyone has something to give, and everyone deserves to be sustained.

**Daily life:** Food, shelter, guidance, and help cultivating a first Morphling make their generosity tangible. People have sincere reasons to join and remain within this community.

**Morphling direction:** Sunburst, a gold-orange lichen crust that spreads across the shoulders and back. Developing layers produce broad fronds that gather sunlight through its green algal partner, feed the host, and pass nourishment on to others; at Primal it anchors as an arbor.

**Hereditary strain: Sunburst** (decided October 20, 2026; replaces Lumenlace)

- **Name:** sunburst lichen is a real orange lichen that crusts coastal rock, and is itself a fungus–alga partnership. That makes it the plainest expression of Marimo's mutual giving. Classification subtitle: *Heliophyta donans*, "giving sun-growth." It replaced an earlier *Xanthomyces donans*, because the *-myces* ending belongs to fungal scars.
- **Aesthetic:** a gold-orange crust across the shoulders and back that spreads into broad fronds and glows in sunlight. It is the bright, open counterpart to Kinbud's crimson mane at the bottom of the gradient.
- **Role:** sustenance, rescue of others, and light.
- **Powers and stage visuals:** see [Sunburst](#sunburst--the-giving-light) under "Strain powers and appearance."

**Mature form:** Living arbors around the lighthouse, with people still recognizable among the spreading fronds. Mature members tend gardens and receive visitors, becoming familiar voices through which newcomers encounter Marimo. The arbors are anchored, not petrified: their members can still withdraw. True petrification into Marimo is a further, separate step.

**Tension:** Someone choosing to withdraw may be understood as abandoning people who depend upon them. Their care is sincere, but their expectations can make departure difficult.

### Ferric — The Holding Stone

**Habitat and settlement:** Rocky shallows, tide pools, and shoreline refuges exposed to waves, changing tides, and debris.

**Belief:** Become someone others can depend upon.

**Daily life:** Maintaining shelter and safe passages is a shared responsibility. Promises carry weight because other people organize their lives around them.

**Morphling direction:** Holdfast, a strain that grows like a kelp holdfast: knotted, root-like gripping masses at the hands and feet that cement to stone. Its layers make the host broader, heavier to move, and increasingly capable of anchoring against stone and current. It grips rather than plates. Morphlings may not grow harvestable plates, shells, or scales, because the armor sets own that material space.

**Mature form:** Great holdfast-rooted beings that brace entrances and shelter pools, cemented to the rock by masses of gripping growth. Their hold is anchoring, not petrification: they can still release and move when needed.

**Relationship with Marimo:** His nourishment supports their community; they maintain shoreline refuges and passages connecting his followers to the sea. Mutual provision is an everyday experience.

**Tension:** Dependability can harden into inflexibility. They may struggle to accept someone leaving an obligation behind, even when circumstances have changed.

### Animus — The Shared Wake

**Habitat and settlement:** Active open water. The traveling school is their home, with sheltered gathering grounds used for raising young, trading, and cultivation.

**Belief:** Strength means keeping pace with one another.

**Daily life:** Families travel together. Experienced swimmers protect the edges, locate food, and help injured or tired members continue. Cooperation is expressed through practiced movement and mutual protection.

**Morphling direction:** Mermaid's Purse, a fast open-water strain. Its layers grow finlets, a crimson lateral shimmer along the flank, a sailfish sail along the spine and at Primal a crescent tail, while flank purses bank blood from the hunt for whoever in the school is hurt worst. Its partner is a blood-digesting gut bacterium.

**Mature form:** Sleek aquatic beings barely recognizable as former humans. Members occupy different positions within the school according to their abilities.

**Relationship with Marimo:** His mutual care is familiar to them, but their roaming life takes them beyond his reach and gives them greater independence than the shoreline houses.

### Ductilis — The Woven Current

**Habitat and settlement:** The working inspiration includes bioluminescent colonies and siphonophore-like forms. Settlements hang between canyon walls on flexible lines, with communal spaces that can separate and reconnect as conditions change.

**Belief:** Connections should be freely made and capable of being released.

**Daily life:** People connect to coordinate work, tend injuries, navigate dangerous water, and lend support. Cooperation is valuable because it can be chosen.

**Morphling direction:** Dead Man's Rope, a branching, slime-mould-like vein network whose light comes from a bacterial partner. Its layers throw ribbon strands that sense the living, catch falls and tether the host to an ally or a block. Members tie strands to share sensation, spread damage and lend blood, and either side can let go.

**Mature form:** An elongated, flowing body with many coordinated branches. It can join a larger living network while retaining the ability to detach.

**Relationship with Marimo:** Direct experience of cooperation makes them sympathetic to him. Their insistence that connections remain adjustable and revocable places limits on that sympathy.

**Neighboring tension:** They may disagree with the Veiled Fold over how much openness trust requires.

### Tenebris — The Veiled Fold

**Habitat and settlement:** Darkness beyond sunlight. Settlements occupy narrow rock clefts, with screened entrances and sheltered chambers.

**Belief:** Belonging does not require surrendering the right to remain unknown.

**Daily life:** Offering shelter does not entitle a host to someone's history, allegiance, or eventual body. Visitors are received by a host; access to one room does not grant access to the entire community.

**Morphling direction:** Ink Cap, a shaggy cap darkened by a black-yeast partner. Its layers hang a translucent black veil from the cap that blurs the host's outline and, at Primal, becomes a featureless hood with only sensory filaments showing. It conceals and escapes in ink; it does not ambush.

**Mature form:** Drifting, shrouded beings whose bodies are difficult to distinguish within the folds. They remain individually conscious.

**Relationship with the two poles:** They resist Marimo's expectations while remaining uneasy about the identity Hydra ultimately asks followers to surrender. They occupy the weakest Hydra association among the deeper houses.

### Congeatio — The Still Bell

**Habitat and settlement:** Cold deep water. A diving bell serves as an enclosed refuge maintained across generations, with careful management of warmth, supplies, and access.

**Belief:** Conserve strength until it is needed.

**Daily life:** Restraint, preparation, and precise action are valued. Hospitality means accepting responsibility for another person's survival, and guests help sustain the refuge. Wasting food or neglecting repairs has serious social consequences.

**Morphling direction:** Hushbell, a folded frost-grey mantle hardened like a dormant sclerotium and kept working in the cold by its bacterial partner. Its layers enclose more of the body, slow hunger and air loss, and close into a sealed bell around a badly hurt host, unfolding for brief periods of activity.

**Mature form:** Sealed, suspended bodies that unfold when needed. Particular elders are awakened for their knowledge or abilities, making those awakenings important communal events.

**Relationship with Hydra:** They admire concentrated strength while questioning the Kindling's appetite for continued growth and consumption.

### Mortem — Last Marrow

**Habitat and settlement:** Whale falls, bones, and the remains that sustain deep-water communities. Homes extend into enormous skeletons as surrounding flesh disappears.

**Belief:** Strength should never go to waste. A lifetime's strength should sustain those who follow.

**Daily life:** A whale fall is food, shelter, and a finite inheritance. Members tend it carefully, recover nourishment from remains, and cultivate organisms within bones. Mobile members care for rooted elders.

**Morphling direction:** Marrowplume, a corpse-feeding strain shaped like a stalked sea lily (see "Real-world references"). Early development produces fine feeding tendrils around the hands and forearms. Later layers spread branching roots and protective sheaths, allowing hosts to anchor into bone while feeding or working.

**Mature form:** Rooted elders housed within hollow bones, with branching roots inside and delicate red feeding plumes extending into the water. Becoming rooted at maturity is an explicitly chosen part of this house's design. Rooting is anchoring, not petrification: it is conscious and reversible.

**Relationship with Hydra:** They spend their lives seeing great bodies become scattered resources. Hydra offers the possibility of cultivated strength remaining joined and purposeful.

**Difference from the Kindling:** Last Marrow can regard sustaining a community as a worthy destination for a body, without requiring eventual incorporation into Hydra.

**Unresolved lifecycle detail:** One suggestion was that elders remain behind as an exhausted whale fall is abandoned, while younger members carry Morphling offspring to the next settlement. The rooted elder concept was agreed. Because rooting is reversible, relocation is possible; whether elders choose to leave or stay, and their final fate, remain open.

### Flammeus — The Kindling

**Habitat and settlement:** Hydrothermal depths and vent terrain. This house has the strongest relationship with Hydra.

**The vents as Hydra's heads:** The house sees the large, towering hydrothermal vents as physical representations of Hydra's heads. Members feed offerings into them throughout their lives. At the end of their lives they enter a vent themselves, which is how they give themselves to Hydra. The vents are the house's central sacred sites and the visible marker of Hydra's presence, counterpart to Marimo's blooms at the surface.

**Belief:** Cultivate the greatest possible version of yourself, then willingly surrender your individuality to strengthen Hydra.

**Fire imagery:** Fire spreads and consumes, erasing distinctions as it transforms kindling into ash and flame regardless of what it once was. The house applies this idea to the eventual surrender of individual identity, and takes its name from it: its members are the kindling that feeds Hydra. Literal flaming bodies are not a requirement of the concept.

**Daily life:** Preparation takes a lifetime. Patience, exacting cultivation, and the development of potential matter. Wasting a person before they reach their potential would squander what they could eventually contribute.

**Morphling direction:** A small, branching polyp bonds along the spine. Early tendrils extend around the ribs and shoulders. Developing layers integrate it with nerves and muscles, progressively reshaping the host toward Hydra's anatomy.

**Proposed defining benefit:** Cultivated regeneration. Injury is repaired by growing tissue that will eventually replace the human form, making healing an early glimpse of the body's destination. Exact mechanics remain undesigned.

**Hereditary strain: Kinbud** (decided October 20, 2026; replaces Emberfang)

- **Name:** a folk name in the Morphling naming register. Real hydras reproduce by budding, growing a new polyp from the parent's body, and the Kindling's life is a long budding toward Hydra. "Kin" carries the house's belief that every member is meant for the one body. Classification subtitle: *Pyrrhiza gemmans*, "budding fire-root," which keeps Emberfang's genus as a trace.
- **Aesthetic:** a fiery lion's mane. The pale polyp along the spine buds at the nape and shoulders, and the buds lengthen into a crown of trailing tentacles that sweeps behind the host like a mane. Colour runs from amber at the root through flame orange to crimson, and the tentacles ripple like flame underwater. The fire is in colour and motion only: nothing literally burns, in keeping with the house's fire-as-erasure metaphor. The mane also suits the stinging crown of Hydra's heads and its sweeping silhouette.
- **Powers and stage visuals:** see [Kinbud](#kinbud--the-kindling) under "Strain powers and appearance." The earlier optional feature-smoothing layer is resolved there as Kinbud's Primal head replacement.

**Mature form:** An independently moving portion of Hydra, barely recognizable as a former person:

- One elongated Hydra head and neck ending in a tentacled polyp crown.
- A distinct pair of tentacle-like arms below the head and neck, separate from the crown tentacles. Their placement supports attacks, handling objects, and familiar gestures.
- Clawlike feet for movement and gripping terrain.
- A long, sweeping tail.

**Hydra's visual reference:** The two supplied rough sketches combine the multiple necks, limbs, and sweeping tail of a mythical Hydra with soft, tentacled polyp heads inspired by the user's aquatic Hydra concept. These sketches establish the anatomical direction for the mature followers as well as Hydra itself.

**Surrender:** At the end of their life, a follower enters a vent to give themselves to Hydra. Their tissues merge into the greater organism, and their individual identity is willingly surrendered. Only the most worthy keep a head: they become another head and neck of Hydra. The rest become part of Hydra's body.

**Maturity and worthiness:** Physical maturity makes surrender possible. It need not compel immediate surrender; transformed elders can remain among the house while continuing their preparation. Since a head is reserved for the most worthy, a lifetime of preparation may still end as body. That tension, along with the one between sincere refinement and attachment to remaining an individual, is available for later development. Who or what judges worthiness remains open.

## Strain powers and appearance

Written October 20, 2026, with a first balance pass the same day. This is the design for what each strain grants while equipped and how it changes the player at each stage. Numbers are calibrated against the current strains (see `docs/archive/done/CURRENT_MORPHLING_STATS_AND_ABILITIES.md`) and the 5,000 mL base blood capacity, following the balance rules below. They still need in-game tuning.

### Shared rules

**Stage ladder (unchanged from the current system).** The passive runs from Unfed and scales with every stage. Developing, Mature and Apex each add one reactive power; Apex's is the strain's signature, with a longer cooldown. Primal is the Morphic Nectar capstone (Apex plus nectar after Apotheos, Degree 8), usually an active power that costs blood, has a cooldown and applies Morphic Strain.

**Home condition.** Each strain has a home condition taken from its house's habitat, where marked powers are stronger or only work. This follows the precedent of Irontooth's below-Y=50 bonuses and Witch's Ear's darkness bonuses, and lets the depth gradient be felt anywhere in the world. Unless a power says otherwise, home condition gives **+50% to durations and rates and +25% to radii**. Only a few powers work *only* at home; each is marked "home only."

| Strain | Home condition |
| --- | --- |
| Sunburst | Daylight with open sky, or water within a few blocks of a daylit surface |
| Holdfast | Standing on or against stone, or submerged |
| Mermaid's Purse | Sprint-swimming, swimming in open ocean (Pelagic Ocean or deeper), or a bloodline ally or tamed companion within 8 blocks |
| Dead Man's Rope | Submerged, or holding at least one link |
| Ink Cap | Light level 7 or lower, or submerged |
| Hushbell | Cold biome, deep water (below sea level and submerged), or standing still |
| Marrowplume | Within 8 blocks of bone blocks or a kill made in the last 30 seconds |
| Kinbud | Near heat: lava, magma, fire, campfires, hot biomes, the Nether, or vents. Reuses the existing `EmberfangHeatRules` |

**Visual stages (unchanged from the current renderer).** Unfed and Fledgling are tint and glow only. Developing shows small protrusions, Mature a larger visible organism, Apex the full authored silhouette, and Primal oversized or replacement anatomy. Attachments use the existing `HEAD`, `BODY`, `ARMS` and `LEGS` anchors. Strains that name two anchors need to be checked against the mutation registry's one-attachment assumption. All effects follow the custom-particle rule: use Hemomancy's glow, cell, tendril and mote toolkit, not vanilla particle clouds.

**Death-saves (resolves the Lumenlace loose end).** Morphlings keep two death-saves, both in the shared Last Rite group:

- **Ink Cap, Apex: Ink Reprieve.** This is Lumenlace's Ink Mantle Reprieve moved with the ink role. It keeps the `hemomancy:ink_mantle` id.
- **Hushbell, Primal: Cryptobiosis.** It stays with its strain and keeps the `hemomancy:cryptobiosis` id.

Lumenlace's Last-Light Mantle (`hemomancy:last_light`) is retired. Migration should clear any armed state. Sunburst gets no self death-save: its rescue is always aimed at others, in keeping with "everyone has something to give."

**Armor and summon boundaries.** No strain grants thorns, on-hit retaliation, toughness or plating; the armor sets own those. Kinbud's Second Head is part of the host's body, not a summon. It does not use the summon registry, cannot be commanded, and does not count toward Puppet Interference. That is the Court's counterpart to Circus puppetry: a body you grow, not one you control.

**Secondary tendencies (proposal).** Each strain's secondary tendency is its neighbouring house toward the middle of the gradient, so the outer houses lean inward and the two middle houses lean on each other.

### Balance rules

These rules set the numbers in the strain tables and should guide later tuning.

1. **Cost bands.** Repeatable Primal powers cost 250–450 mL (5–9% of the 5,000 mL base) on 12–90 second cooldowns. The two death-saves cost 500–650 mL and share the Last Rite group's 10-minute cooldown. Passive upkeep stays at the configured baseline (about 10 mL per minute).
2. **Cooldown bands.** Developing powers recover in 3–15 seconds, Mature in 8–20, Apex in 20–60, and Primal in 12–90. Always-on conditional powers (Cling, Lantern Fronds, Folded Away) have no cooldown but end when their condition does.
3. **Solo play.** Every Developing, Mature and Apex power must do something for a host with no allies or companions. Group powers carry a solo fallback, marked "Alone." Social strains should be better in a group, never useless without one.
4. **Never lethal to the giver.** Health moved, shared or redirected to protect others never takes the giver below a floor (50% for Shared Table and Colony, 30% for Breakwater). Protecting someone must not kill the protector.
5. **Shared caps.** All Morphling healing, including Kinbud's regrowth, counts toward the existing passive-income circulation cap. Knockback resistance respects the triad attribute caps. Resistance from different sources does not stack amplifiers; the highest applies.
6. **No undead immunity by accident.** Kinbud's sting is a custom **Stung** effect rather than Poison, so it affects undead and isn't cleansed by poison immunity.
7. **Economy guard.** Bonus-loot powers exclude players, bosses and spawner-flagged mobs, and roll by chance rather than on every kill.

**Main changes from the first draft:**

- **Lower or narrower:**
  - Holdfast's mining bonus is now stone-only and lower than Irontooth's.
  - Ink Cap's always-on detection reduction is lowered and capped at 50% even at home.
  - Marrowplume's home bonus is ×1.5, not doubled.
  - Fall's Inheritance is a 25–40% chance, not every kill.
- **Limited:**
  - Red Rope's cooldown rises from 8 to 12 seconds, and it no longer poisons.
  - Kindled's full regrowth is capped at 20 HP.
  - Deliquesce now blocks attacking.
- **Solo fallbacks added:** Shared Table, Breakwater, Slipstream, Strand Link (a block grapple) and Root Network.
- **Stronger:** Sun-Fed now also turns surplus sunlight into blood.

### Classification names

The Order's pseudo-Latin names appear only as a greyed tooltip subtitle and a Bestiary classification line. Every current Morphling genus uses a plant-, spore- or root-style ending (*-phyta*, *-spora*, *-rhiza*, *-bursa*, *-aria*, *-morpha*), while fungal scars use *-myces* (*Thanomyces*, *Antiphonomyces*). The new names keep that split, so a player can tell a raised strain from a refined scar by its Latin alone. Where a strain inherits most of its predecessor's mechanics, it keeps the old genus as a trace.

| Strain | Classification | Meaning | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sunburst | *Heliophyta donans* | "Giving sun-growth" | Replaces the earlier *Xanthomyces donans*, whose *-myces* ending belongs to fungal scars |
| Holdfast | *Hapterophyta tenax* | "Tenacious gripping-growth" | Haptera are the gripping fingers of a real kelp holdfast |

### Real-world references

Each strain draws on three real-world references, following the existing reframe ("a fungal strain that has convergently evolved an animal-like solution"):

- **Fungus:** what the strain *is*, its tissue and growth habit.
- **Convergent form:** the body solution it imitates, for silhouette and behaviour. It resembles that creature without being it.
- **Partner:** the real symbiosis behind the partner-organism theme.

Convergent forms avoid creatures that already exist in the mod as mobs, growths or armor materials, so a strain never reads as a tamed copy of something the player can meet. The "Avoid" column lists the nearest existing creatures to stay visually distinct from.

**Notes.**

- **Changes to the earlier picks:**
  - Dead Man's Rope no longer imitates siphonophores, because the Siphonophore now exists as a Twilight Ocean mob.
  - Marrowplume no longer imitates Osedax, because Bone Worm colonies already fill that role in the Carrion Depths. The stalked sea lily fits better anyway: it is anchored but can let go.
  - The Woven Current's lore can still say they live among siphonophores. Only the strain avoids copying them.
- **Look over name:** honey fungus and corpse-finder fungi are plain brown mushrooms that would look alike as sprites. They stay as naming and theme references, while Dead Man's Rope takes its look from slime-mould veins and Marrowplume from the bleeding tooth fungus and scarlet club.
- **Shared real names:** the octopus stinkhorn's common folk name begins with "devil's"; reference the organism, not that name, to stay within the lore guardrail. Likewise, don't use the slime mould's Latin epithet *polycephalum* ("many-headed") in game, since many heads belong to Hydra. "Marrowplume" also sits close to the existing Marrow Crown helmet; the two should look and read differently.

### Implementation notes

- **Renames and migration:** all eight item ids, effects, incubator recipes, models, textures, lang entries, Bestiary entries and HUD sprites need renaming. Saved strain items should migrate through `MorphlingMigrationRules`, and a retired Last-Light Mantle's armed state must be cleared.
- **New render geometry:** every strain needs new attachments, and Holdfast, Mermaid's Purse, Dead Man's Rope, Ink Cap, Marrowplume and Kinbud use two anchors. The current registry pairs each strain with one attachment.
- **New mechanics:** detection range (Ink Cap), damage redirection (Holdfast), shared damage (Dead Man's Rope), queued regrowth (Kinbud) and the Second Head body attachment that strikes.
- **Reused mechanics:** most other powers are recast existing code paths, which keeps the rewrite manageable.
- **Docs to update with the implementation:** `docs/HEMOMANCY_REFERENCE.md` §16, `docs/LORE_REFERENCE.md`, the current-stats reference, and the Mycophant Tendril references to the "fungal Morphling head."

## In-game presence

Written October 20, 2026. This covers how the houses appear in the world and how the player first learns of the Court.

### Houses mapped onto the existing ocean layers

The Pelagic worldgen already stacks distinct biomes down the water column (`docs/PELAGIC_WORLDGEN.md`), and they line up with the house gradient.

| House | Biome | Depth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Holding Stone | Rockpool Shore | Coast, tide pools |
| The Giving Light | Erythrocoral Reef and the coast beside it | Sunlit shelf |
| The Shared Wake | Pelagic Ocean | Open water |
| The Woven Current | Twilight Ocean | Y 33 to 13, gullies |
| The Veiled Fold | Midnight Ocean beneath temperate or warm surface water | Y 12 to -8, canyon clefts |
| The Still Bell | Midnight Ocean beneath cold or frozen surface water | Y 12 to -8 |
| Last Marrow | Carrion Depths | Y -9 to -30, sediment fans, fossil beds |
| The Kindling | Hydrothermal Depths | Y -31 to -53, vent fields |

That gives seven layers for eight houses. The Veiled Fold and the Still Bell share Midnight and are split by the climate of the surface above them, so they never meet. This fits the Still Bell's cold-deep-water habitat.

### Three tiers of presence

To keep any stretch of ocean from filling with villages, the houses appear at three scales.

1. **Seats: two full settlements, rare.** Only the outer houses get them, matching their lore depth.
   - **The Lighthouse Garden** (Giving Light): a lighthouse on the coast, terraced gardens running into the reef, and anchored arbor members. It is the Court's welcoming face and the D5 destination.
   - **The Vent Sanctum** (Kindling): built around one of the hydrothermal vent fields the terrain already seeds, whose chimneys are Hydra's heads. It should anchor to the seeded vent-field centres rather than random positions, so it always sits on a real vent mound.
2. **Outposts: one small structure per inner house**, two or three buildings at most.
   - **Holding Stone, Tide Refuge:** a sheltered pool behind a holdfast-braced entrance.
   - **Shared Wake, no fixed structure:** a roaming school of members, which is literally their lore. At most a rare, small nursery ground.
   - **Woven Current, Line Camp:** pods and nets strung across a gully, able to separate and reconnect.
   - **Veiled Fold, Screened Cleft:** a doorway into a canyon wall, almost invisible from outside.
   - **Still Bell, The Bell:** one sealed diving bell with an air pocket inside, which doubles as a deep rest stop.
   - **Last Marrow, Whale Fall:** one great skeleton with rooted elders in it.
3. **Signs: small features, frequent.** Sunburst blooms on the surface, relay markers, messenger buoys and house boundary markers tell the player whose waters they are in without placing a settlement.

### Placement rules

- **All outposts share one structure set.** Vanilla generates at most one structure from a set per placement cell, choosing among the entries valid there. So outposts can never stack vertically over the same patch of seabed: one cell holds one Court outpost, whatever its depth. Starting point: `random_spread` spacing 28, separation 10, tuned on the review seeds.
- **Seats use their own sparse set**, starting at spacing 64 (about 1,000 blocks), with an exclusion zone that keeps outposts away from them.
- **Exclusion zones** against the Circus pavilion, troupe camps, Voyager wrecks and vessels, and ocean monuments, so no ocean view holds more than one faction's landmark.
- **Underwater fog does the rest.** Deep outposts are never visible together. Only the Lighthouse Garden and Tide Refuge are visible from the surface, and they are the rarest.
- **Keep placement cheap.** The Circus pavilion's footprint check once took 47% of the worldgen thread. Court structures should run the cheap biome and depth predicate first, and the Vent Sanctum should reuse the terrain's seeded vent centres.
- **Choose generation height deliberately.** Each depth biome is only about 20 blocks tall, so each structure must pick its Y to land inside the right layer, especially the Veiled Fold and Still Bell.

### How the player learns of the Court

Three routes, any of which can come first. As with the Circus, they converge on one server-authoritative "Court known" state. Whichever route arrives later acknowledges the earlier discovery instead of repeating it. The Court stays visitable before D5, without being presented as a lesson.

1. **The Vicar and the Voyagers.** At D5 the Vicar refers the player to a Harbinger Voyager, building on the D3 Side assignment *The Reef Watch*, which already connects the two. Voyagers are field researchers who have dealt with the Court at a cordial distance; they measure and witness where the Court cultivates. The Voyager issues a reissuable **Relay Chart** (working name) toward the nearest Lighthouse Garden, the Court's equivalent of the Circus's Scarlet Waybill.
2. **A Brined Votary.** Brined Votaries are drowned Harbinger remnants guarding Voyager wrecks, and those wrecks generate in the Erythrocoral Reef, the same layer as the Lighthouse Garden. One explanation: some wrecks were failed attempts to reach the Court, and their drowned crews are would-be envoys. A Votary can carry an undelivered **Brine-Sealed Letter** (working name), a letter of introduction sealed with Sunburst crust. To keep the Votary from becoming a farming target, the letter drops only for a player who doesn't yet know the Court, once. Item inquiries with the Vicar, Alchemist and Voyager explain it and record discovery.
3. **The Alchemist.** The Alchemist already runs Morphling capture and the Living Bestiary. Since every strain is a house's hereditary line, the Order's Latin classifications are an outsider's catalogue of strains the Court raises. Once the player is D5 and has a strain at Mature or higher, the Alchemist can say plainly that the best cultivators of these lines are not in the Order, and point the player toward the Voyagers or the coast.

**Lore impact.** Route 2 gives the Brined Votary a Court connection. `docs/LORE_REFERENCE.md` currently says reef ecology "establishes no new doctrine for the Brined Votary," so that line would change. It also explains the shared word: "Brined" may be what Voyagers call anything that came back from the Court's waters, whether a drowned envoy or the Court itself.

**Locating.** A vanilla `/locate` of the Circus pavilion once took 209 seconds. The Relay Chart's search should be bounded and run off the main thread, or use precomputed candidates, so issuing it never stalls the server.

## Communication between houses

### Agreed direction

Communication should use animal or Morphling messengers, some form of bloody currents for long-distance transmission, or a combination of the two. The exact operation of the currents has not been settled.

### Proposed implementation of that idea — still open

The following division of roles was suggested after that direction was stated. It has not yet been explicitly confirmed:

- **Animal and Morphling messengers:** Carry precise messages, small objects, and private correspondence. Relay shelters allow a delivery to pass between organisms suited to different depths.
- **Blood currents:** Carry recognizable hematic signals through established waterways, including warnings, invitations, requests for contact, or notice of a closed passage.
- **Limits:** Detailed exchanges require a messenger or meeting, preserving the significance of distance and habitat boundaries.
- **Shared maintenance:** Houses maintain their own relays and receiving routes. Territorial control includes deciding whether a route continues into a house's waters.
- **Possible Court obligation:** Maintain relays and answer distress signals despite political disagreement.
- **Possible Woven Current role:** Particular expertise in tending connections, without exclusive control over the network.

These are setting and gameplay possibilities, not a specified transport or signaling system.

## What is ready and what remains open

The current design is sufficient to guide further house development. All eight have working names, habitat directions, cultural principles, and Morphling transformation concepts. The Court has a shared historical and practical basis, and its allegiance gradient has been established.

### Decided October 20, 2026

- The Flammeus house is renamed **The Kindling**.
- **Anchoring** (conscious, reversible) is distinct from **true petrification** (permanent incorporation).
- **Hydra's heads:** only the most worthy keep a head; the rest become body.
