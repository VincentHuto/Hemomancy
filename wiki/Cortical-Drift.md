# Cortical Drift

The Cortical Drift is an End biome built around one thing: a brain the size of a small island, hollow, wired inside with nerve fiber, and quietly arcing lightning between its own synapses years after whatever occupied it went still. Two broken, gently tilted rings of smaller islands orbit that Vagrant Mind. They are strung to one another by sagging cable bridges anchored in the same black-blue-yellow-white tissue the brain is made of, so the whole formation reads as one nervous system held in orbit rather than unrelated landmarks scattered through the void.

It's also the Ferric Ductilis school's natural home turf: the bridge network is wired for conduction, not just decoration.

## How to find it

Cortical Drift generates in the End among the scattered outer islands — it's woven into TerraBlender's outer-End tables rather than living in its own pocket dimension. You will not stumble into a complete formation beside the dragon fight. Each generated archipelago takes its actual Vagrant Mind placement as its centre, then grows two broken rings of small flat-topped islands around it: bone-white crust over deep blue stone, yellow ganglion seams, tapering undersides hung with stalactite spikes, and nerve cables sagging around the orbit through floating ganglion knots. The archipelago feature is invoked throughout the End and performs its own Mind-candidate check, allowing one orbital system to cross biome borders without islands or cables being sliced at chunk edges.

Whether a given world generates any Cortical Drift at all, and how common it is, is controlled by server config (`enableCorticalDriftEndRegion` and `corticalDriftEndRegionWeight`) and requires a restart to change.

## Blocks

The biome's terrain and structures are built from ten blocks that function as a shared material language rather than random decoration — you'll see the same four colors everywhere, from the ground under your feet to the brain's shell to the bridge anchors:

| Block | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Neural Tissue** | The black bulk stone — deep strata and the core of every bridge anchor |
| **Axonal Slate** | Deep blue banded stone just under the surface crust |
| **Myelin Sheath** | The white/bone surface crust you actually walk on |
| **Ganglion Matter** | Yellow, faintly glowing (light level 6) — seams in the crust and anchor cores |
| **Cortical Folds** | Ridged gray-white skin, used only on the Vagrant Mind's exterior |
| **Nerve Fiber** | Thin cable segment, oriented along its run |
| **Nerve Bundle** | Thick, multi-strand cable segment for the heavier bridges |
| **Synaptic Node** | White, brightly lit (light level 10) — every branch point and bridge terminus, and the block that makes the whole network conduct |
| **Dura Membrane** | Translucent skin stretched over cavity openings |
| **Dendrite Growth** | Small decorative cross-model growth scattered on the surface |

None of these have special drops beyond themselves — they're a building and crafting material, not a farming target. A heads-up: the current textures are early placeholders and are due for a proper hand-drawn pass, so don't read the art as final.

## The Vagrant Mind

At the centre of each archipelago sits the Vagrant Mind: one colossal brain, roughly 150 to 250 blocks across, grown rather than built — nobody constructed it, and no faction claims to know who or what it belonged to. Minds keep their normal structure placement frequency even when a natural End island crosses their space. During generation, only intersecting vanilla End Stone inside a slightly expanded version of the Mind's joined-lobe silhouette is opened to air before the organ is placed. The irregular clearance follows the cerebrum, cerebellum, and brainstem rather than cutting a box or sphere, leaving an organic terrain lip around the finished structure while protecting authored blocks and unrelated structures. Its exterior is a ridged shell of gyri divided by a gently wandering sagittal sulcus of exposed neural tissue. The seam remains closed instead of opening a straight window into the hollow interior. Beneath the rear cerebrum, the paired cerebellar hemispheres have a narrow median cleft and their own crosswise folia, making them read as two folded lobes. They taper into an elongated, narrow brainstem that descends well below the rest of the organ, giving the underside a distinct silhouette instead of one rounded cerebral lump. Inside, it's hollow, and the cavity is fully explorable: a web of nerve fiber and nerve bundle is strung between anchor points across the inner shell, meeting at synaptic nodes that still spark with lightning on their own, unprompted, the same way the bridges outside do.

It's worth the detour. The fissure entrance is usually visible from a distance once you know to look for it, and the interior is one of the biome's few enclosed, walkable spaces rather than open void terrain.

## The bridge network

Every island in a given stretch of the Drift is connected to at least one other by a sagging fiber-cable bridge; a few well-placed hub islands carry three to five bundles at once, which is usually a sign you've found a more important junction. Where a cable lands, expect a knot of neural tissue and axonal slate shot through with ganglion matter, capped by a bright synaptic node — that's the anchor, and it's a reliable place to look for the next bridge onward.

One honest limitation: the network doesn't stretch forever. Each orbital system is planned from one Vagrant Mind placement, so its islands are wired locally rather than being connected to the next Mind's orbit across the void.

## Ductilis synergy

This is the payoff for Ferric Ductilis players: synaptic nodes are registered as conductors, so conduction-based manipulations — Storm and its kin — chain along the bridge network exactly like they would through wired-up iron construction blocks. Standing on one island and triggering a conduction cast can carry current down a bridge, into a hub, and out along every bundle it connects to, node by node, with no special setup required. Between that and the Vagrant Mind's own internal web, the Cortical Drift is built to be fought and cast in, not just looked at.

## Myelin Borers

Something lives on the cables. Myelin Borers are blind, segmented grubs that scrunch along exposed
nerve fibers and connections rather than walking the islands. New Vagrant Minds place a few on the
middle of their interior web, and natural spawns require a cable directly underfoot. Most of the time
they are busy repairing: find a span with a chunk missing and, given a little time, a Borer will close
it back up. Pale gold light gathers around the repair; red sparks and fragments mark one chewing a cable.
When a route crosses a missing section, the grub vanishes into a red-gold ball of Ductilis lightning
and zaps to a nearby cable before reforming.

They will not bother you unless you bother the network. Break a fiber, bundle or synaptic node near
one, or hit a Borer, and every Borer within sixteen blocks turns: they start tearing cables out of the
bridges instead of mending them, and they will come after you, biting with Neural Overload that stacks
if they land repeated hits. Leave them alone for about thirty seconds and they calm down and start
repairing the damage. They never eat synaptic nodes, so the hubs and anchors always survive and a
wrecked stretch of bridge can always grow back.

Killing one drops **Ganglion Clusters**. Four of them around a Myelin Sheath crafts a **Synaptic
Node** — and since synaptic nodes are ductilis conductors, that is how you extend the conduction
network yourself: build your own junctions and cable runs and chain Ferric Ductilis casts wherever you
want them.

The practical advice is simple. If you want the bridges intact for travelling, mine somewhere else.

## Naeglerophaeon

A deep-maroon cluster of luminous red and yellow orbs swims freely through the Vagrant Mind.
Twelve ivory tendrils trail behind **Naeglerophaeon**, joined near their roots by thin red membranes.
Pulses travel outward through the tendril nodes and spark at their tips. A much longer crimson
capture tendril carries steady yellow-white lights. While roaming, the tendrils open slowly and
snap inward together to propel it. While tracking prey, they tuck close as its body sways from
side to side. When it turns, the outer tendril joints and long crimson tail follow through after
the core instead of rotating as a rigid cluster.

It also tends the web. When a Myelin Borer chews a repairable one-block gap, Naeglerophaeon
may leave its patrol to close it with yellow-white Ductilis lightning. Breaking a fiber or bundle
yourself gets its attention even during a fight: it abandons its current attack, swims to the cut,
and spends a short time repairing it. It will still investigate a larger cut, but only gaps held
between opposing nerve blocks can regrow; tearing away an entire span does not create a new bridge
through empty space.

The core brightens for one second before firing red-purple lightning. Solid terrain blocks the shot.
When you come within twelve blocks, its long tendril can swing forward, wrap your torso, and reel
you toward the core over five seconds. Your view grows darker and redder as you approach, while
the core's eyes shine yellow through it. The color cuts off when the hold ends, as Ductilis lightning
bursts outward from your body. Strike the gripping section or the core: eight damage breaks the
hold, and allies can help. You can still look around and attack while held. Reaching the core
causes a four-heart discharge before armor, then throws you away; this attack can kill you.
Terrain that blocks the pull breaks the hold instead of dragging you through blocks.

The Mind's resident does not respawn after defeat and does not appear in Peaceful mode.
Minds generated before this encounter was added can still house one.

Defeating it drops one **Naeglerophaeon Ganglion**. Craft it with two Ganglion Clusters and a Ferric
Binder to make **Synaptic Step**. Stand within three blocks of any synaptic node, aim at a visible
node no more than 32 blocks from the source, and use the tool to blink to the top of that node.
Generated and player-built nodes both work, in any dimension. The destination must be loaded and
have room for you; a successful jump has a ten-second cooldown. Neither the tool nor the nodes are
consumed.
